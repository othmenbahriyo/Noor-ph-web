## The place of Quran teaching in Islamic upbringing

Upbringing in Muslim society is distinguished by an approach that carefully builds the child's character, both outwardly (behavior, ethics) and inwardly (belief, religion). Among the most important teachings passed down by Islam is raising the child with the Quran from the earliest age.

According to Abu Hurayra (may Allah be pleased with him), the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever learns the Quran in his youth, the Quran mixes with his flesh and blood" (narrated by al-Bayhaqi). Attachment to the Quran from an early age makes it an integral part of the child's formation, rather than mere knowledge memorized in passing.

In Sahih al-Bukhari, according to Uthman ibn Affan (may Allah be pleased with him), the Prophet ﷺ said: "The best among you is the one who learns the Quran and teaches it." This applies to children even before adults — they deserve special attention due to how quickly they memorize and the purity of their hearts.

## Scholars' recommendations on teaching the Quran to children

Throughout the centuries, scholars have stressed the importance of beginning with the Quran in the upbringing of children:

- Al-Hafiz as-Suyuti said: "Teaching children the Quran is a foundation of Islam: they grow up upon the Fitra (the pure natural disposition), and the lights of wisdom reach their hearts before desires can seize them."
- Ibn Khaldun wrote in his Muqaddimah: "The teaching of the Quran by parents is one of the symbols of religion... and the Quran has become the foundation of education upon which all other skills are subsequently built."
- Ibn Sina (Avicenna) said: "When the child is ready to receive instruction and his hearing has awakened, one begins by teaching him the Quran, introducing him to the letters of the alphabet, and instilling in him the principles of religion."

Ibn Kathir reports that Ibn Abbas (may Allah be pleased with them) used to say: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ passed away when I was ten years old, and I had already read the Muhkam (the clear surahs of the Quran)." He comments that memorizing the Quran in childhood is more solid and more lasting in memory than memorizing it in adulthood.

## How does the Quran influence a child's soul?

The Quran has a profound effect on the human soul in general, and the child, through the purity of his nature, is especially receptive to this effect. This is why the short surahs (such as those of the last Juz) were revealed with few words, close rhymes, and a sound rhythm suited to the child's short breath and his capacity for repetition and memorization.

This correspondence between the structure of the short surahs and the child's abilities is no coincidence: it reflects the divine wisdom that made the Quran easy to memorize, as many scholars and learned men have pointed out throughout history.

## The effect of teaching the Quran on society

The merit of teaching the Quran to children is not limited to the individual alone: in the Islamic view, it extends to society as a whole — the spread of Quran learning and recitation being considered a source of general blessing.

## Inspiring examples of young Quran memorizers

The scholars of Islam have given the finest examples of memorizing the Quran from a young age:

- Imam ash-Shafi'i said: "I memorized the Quran at the age of seven."
- Sahl at-Tustari said: "I learned the Quran and memorized it at the age of six or seven."
- Ibn Sina had fully mastered the Quran by the age of ten.
- Ibn al-Jazari had memorized the seven readings (Qira'at) in addition to the Quran by the age of seventeen.

These examples confirm that memorizing the Quran at a young age is not an exception, but rather the path taken by many great scholars.

## How to make learning the Quran easy and enjoyable for children today

With the evolution of educational tools, it is now possible to combine traditional teaching methods with modern tools to facilitate a child's memorization of the Quran:

- **Repeated listening** to a clear recitation helps the child absorb correct pronunciation even before memorizing.
- **Interactive repetition**, breaking memorization into short passages with small rewards, keeps the child's motivation alive.
- **Regular daily practice**, even with just a few verses, is more effective than long, spaced-out sessions.

## Conclusion

Raising children in the shade of the Quran is not just about memorizing words: it is a purification of the soul and an anchoring of values from the earliest age. Every step, however small, in teaching a child the Book of Allah is an investment in his spiritual and moral future.
