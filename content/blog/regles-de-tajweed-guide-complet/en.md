## What is Tajweed?

**Tajweed** (تجويد) refers to the set of pronunciation rules that allow the Quran to be recited the way it was revealed. The word itself means "to improve" or "to make better": it's not just about reading the Arabic letters, but about respecting the length of sounds, the connections between letters, and the correct pauses.

Learning Tajweed is an essential step for any Muslim who wants to recite the Quran correctly, whether Arabic is their native language or not.

## Articulation Points of the Letters (Makharij al-Huruf)

Every Arabic letter has a precise point of origin in the mouth or throat, called a **makhraj** (plural: *makharij*). There are five main zones:

1. **The throat (al-Halq)** — letters like ء، ه، ع، ح، غ، خ
2. **The tongue (al-Lisan)** — the majority of Arabic letters, depending on the exact position of the tongue
3. **The lips (ash-Shafatan)** — letters like ب، م، و، ف
4. **The nasal cavity (al-Khayshum)** — for nasal resonance (ghunnah)
5. **The oral cavity (al-Jawf)** — for long vowels

Mastering these articulation points avoids confusing similar-sounding letters (such as ذ and ز, or ط and ت), which can change the meaning of a word.

## Noun Sakinah and Tanween

**Noun Sakinah** (نون ساكنة) is a noon without a vowel, and **Tanween** (تنوين) corresponds to the double final vowels (ً ٍ ٌ). These two cases follow four rules depending on the letter that follows:

- **Al-Idhhar** (الإظهار): clear pronunciation, without modification
- **Al-Idgham** (الإدغام): merging with the following letter
- **Al-Iqlab** (الإقلاب): transforming the sound into an "m"
- **Al-Ikhfa** (الإخفاء): partial concealment of the sound, with nasal resonance

## Waqf (Stopping) and Ibtida (Resuming) Rules

**Waqf** indicates where it is possible or recommended to pause while reading, without changing the meaning of the verse. The most common symbols are:

- **م (Waqf Lazim)**: mandatory stop
- **لا**: do not stop here
- **ج (Waqf Ja'iz)**: permitted stop
- **قلى**: preferable stop

Respecting these indications ensures that the meaning of the text remains intact, especially when a verse contains several linked clauses.

## How to Practice Tajweed Every Day

The best way to improve is to combine several approaches:

- **Listen to renowned reciters** to get your ear used to correct sounds
- **Record yourself** and compare your recitation to a reference
- **Use phonetic transcription and Tajweed color-coding** to visualize each rule directly on the text
- **Get regular, ideally personalized, corrections** to identify recurring mistakes
